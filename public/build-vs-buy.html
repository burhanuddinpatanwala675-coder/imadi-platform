Build vs. buy: a practical framework.

Every growing business eventually asks the same question about a piece of software or a workflow: do we buy something off the shelf, or build it ourselves? Here's how we think it through with clients.

Start with the real question

"Build vs. buy" is usually asked too early. The more useful question first is: how core is this capability to what makes your business different? A tool that handles something every business needs — accounting, email, scheduling — is rarely worth building from scratch. A workflow that reflects exactly how your business wins is a different story.

When buying usually wins

  • The need is common. If dozens of mature products already solve this problem well, you're unlikely to build something better for less.
  • Speed matters more than fit. You need something working this month, not this year.
  • The workflow is standard. Your process doesn't meaningfully differ from how most businesses in your space operate.
  • You don't want ongoing ownership. Buying shifts maintenance, security patching, and uptime onto the vendor.

When building usually wins

  • It's core to your advantage. The workflow, data, or customer experience is part of what actually differentiates you.
  • Off-the-shelf tools force compromise. You're bending your process to fit the software, not the other way around, and that's costing you.
  • You're paying twice. You already own several tools and are manually connecting them — a sign the real need is integration, not another subscription.
  • Data ownership and control matter. You need full visibility into how a process works and where your data lives.

How AI changes the calculation

AI has quietly moved a meaningful slice of the "build" column into the realm of realistic. Custom AI agents, document processing, and workflow automation used to require a large engineering team to build well. That's no longer strictly true — a focused, well-scoped AI system can now be built faster and more affordably than a broad off-the-shelf platform that only fits 70% of what you actually need.

That doesn't mean "build everything with AI." It means the honest comparison has shifted: a narrowly-built AI solution designed around your exact workflow is now a realistic competitor to a generic SaaS subscription — not just a large-company luxury.

A short way to decide

If you can answer "yes" to most of these, building is worth seriously evaluating: this workflow is close to what makes us different; existing tools force real compromises; we're stitching together multiple tools already; and we're prepared to own what we build (or have a partner who will). If most answers are "no," buying is very likely the faster, cheaper, lower-risk path — at least for now.
